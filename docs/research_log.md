\# ACE Atlas — Research Log



\## Core Hypothesis



Semantic stability emerges from contextual geometric fields constructed from invariant semantic anchors.



Language reliability can be estimated through:



\- origin cost

\- field competition

\- margin

\- density

\- stability index



instead of probabilistic confidence alone.



\---



\# Major Discoveries



\## 1. Fields are necessary



Low stability was not primarily caused by:

\- router failures,

\- thresholds,

\- or margin logic.



Instead, instability emerged from incomplete contextual geometry.



Adding:

\- scientific

\- legal

\- business



significantly improved factual and overlap stability.



\---



\## 2. Overlap is legitimate



Semantic overlap is not necessarily ambiguity.



Examples:

\- business ↔ legal

\- conceptual ↔ narrative

\- scientific ↔ operational



Overlap often reflects real contextual coexistence.



\---



\## 3. Low-context language is structurally unstable



Low-context sentences remain unstable even after expanding the Atlas.



This suggests:

\- low-context instability is structural,

\- not contextual.



\---



\## 4. Contradiction survives geometry



Contradictory statements may still exhibit:

\- coherent syntax,

\- semantic density,

\- low origin cost.



Therefore:

contradiction detection requires additional coherence evaluation beyond geometry alone.



\---



\## 5. The Atlas emerges geometrically



The system is no longer behaving as:

\- keyword classification,

\- intent detection,

\- routing heuristics.



Instead:

semantic fields emerge as geometric contextual attractors.
